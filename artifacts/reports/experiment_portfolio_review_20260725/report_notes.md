# Supporting notes: experiment portfolio review

## Decision frame

The report advises how to turn the current UK music-cities project into a
sequence of credible publications. It distinguishes evidence that can support
the first public essay now from exploratory results that should define the
research design for later studies.

## Editorial scoring rubric

The 1–5 scores are structured editorial judgments, not statistical estimates.

- **Evidence readiness:** reproducibility, source coverage, alignment between
  design and claim, and whether current caveats permit public use.
- **Publication upside:** ability to support a distinctive question with a
  coherent human and methodological story after the stated next work.
- **Surprise value:** how much the result changes the project's interpretation
  rather than merely confirming scale or an expected city ordering.

The ratings were assigned after inspecting the executed notebooks, exported
coverage summaries, the final notebook, `POST.md`, and `STUDY_REVIEW_1.md`.

## Validation notes

- The numerical claims in the evidence matrix were reconciled to notebook
  `tl;dr` and takeaway cells and to the saved coverage summaries.
- Population-sensitive claims use the frozen 2024 OECD FUA observations. The
  Spotify numerator is frozen in 2026, so those claims retain the documented
  two-year numerator/denominator mismatch.
- Experiment 18 and experiment 19 use different catalogues and different
  upstream sources; their coverage rates must not be presented as a direct
  source benchmark without a matched-record audit.
- Correlations in experiments 20, 22 and 23 are descriptive. None supports a
  causal interpretation.
- The recommended study sequence is an editorial inference from the evidence,
  not an output computed by the notebooks.
